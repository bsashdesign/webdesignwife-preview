Web Design Wife vs. Framer: an honest comparison

By Ben Sash · 2 min read

The short answer: Framer is a favorite of designers and startups for bold, animated websites. It's powerful, but it's aimed at design-savvy people, not busy business owners who want their site handled.

What Framer does well

  • Beautiful animations and modern layouts
  • Fast to build if you already know design tools
  • AI features for generating page layouts
  • Hosting included

Where it can fall short for a local business

  • Built for designers and startups, not local service businesses
  • Easy to prioritize flashy effects over clear information
  • You're still maintaining it yourself

Choose Framer if…

…you're comfortable with design tools and want a striking portfolio or startup site. That's a perfectly good reason, and plenty of local businesses do well with it.

Choose a managed website if…

…you want a clear site that brings in local customers, without learning a design tool.

What it really costs

Plans scale with traffic and features. Budget for a designer's time if you want changes you can't make yourself. With any do-it-yourself builder, the biggest cost is usually time. A few hours a month on updates, fixes and design tweaks adds up to several full days a year.

If you want to switch later

Framer sites live on Framer. Moving means rebuilding the design somewhere else, since the site can't be exported as a whole. Whatever you choose, make sure your domain name is registered in your own name. That's what lets you move without losing your web address.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Do I enjoy working on my website, or do I put it off?
  • When did I last update my hours, prices or photos?
  • Do I need to sell online, or do I mainly need customers to call, book or visit?
  • How much is an hour of my time worth to my business?

Going with Framer? Our tips

  1. Make sure your phone number and hours are easy to find
  2. Use animation sparingly so the site stays fast
  3. Test on older phones, not just new ones
  4. Fill in page titles and descriptions for search
  5. Keep the most important action in the first screen
