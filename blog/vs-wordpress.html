Web Design Wife vs. WordPress: An Honest Comparison

By Ben Sash, 3 min read

The short answer: WordPress is incredibly flexible and powers a huge share of the web. It's a great choice if you have a developer or technical skills. Without one, the ongoing maintenance often becomes a burden for a small business.

What WordPress Does Well

  • Extremely flexible, with thousands of themes and plugins
  • You can own and move every part of the site
  • Great for large sites with lots of content
  • A huge community, so help is easy to find

Where It Can Fall Short for a Local Business

  • WordPress core, themes and plugins need regular updates
  • Sites that fall behind on updates are a common target for hackers
  • Plugins can conflict with each other and break things
  • You need to arrange hosting, backups and security yourself

Choose WordPress If…

…you have a developer, or you're comfortable with the technical side. That's a perfectly good reason, and plenty of local businesses do well with it.

Choose a Managed Website If…

…you want a site that stays updated and secure without you thinking about it.

What It Really Costs

The software is free, but hosting, premium themes, plugins, security and a developer's time for updates and fixes all add up. With any do-it-yourself builder, the biggest cost is usually time. A few hours a month on updates, fixes and design tweaks comes to several full days a year.

If You Want to Switch Later

WordPress is the easiest to move: the whole site can be copied to a new host. That freedom is a real advantage if you have someone technical to help. Whatever you choose, make sure your domain name is registered in your own name. That's what lets you move without losing your web address.

Questions to Ask Yourself

  • Do I enjoy working on my website, or do I put it off?
  • When did I last update my hours, prices or photos?
  • Do I need to sell online, or do I mainly need customers to call, book or visit?
  • How much is an hour of my time worth to my business?

Going with WordPress? My Tips

  1. Choose a managed WordPress host that handles backups and updates
  2. Turn on automatic updates wherever you can
  3. Use as few plugins as possible, and only well-maintained ones
  4. Add a security plugin and use strong, unique passwords
  5. Check your site on your phone after every update
