Web Design Wife vs. Hostinger Website Builder: An Honest Comparison

By Ben Sash · 2 min read

The short answer: Hostinger's website builder is an affordable do-it-yourself option with AI tools to speed things up. It's a reasonable choice on a tight budget if you're comfortable doing the work.

What Hostinger Website Builder Does Well

  • Budget-friendly, with hosting included
  • AI tools to generate layouts and text
  • Simple drag-and-drop editing
  • Domain and email options in one place

Where It Can Fall Short for a Local Business

  • Templates can feel basic
  • AI-generated content needs real editing to sound like you
  • Introductory prices often renew higher
  • Every update is still your job

Choose Hostinger Website Builder If…

…you want a low-cost DIY site and don't mind doing the updates. That's a perfectly good reason, and plenty of local businesses do well with it.

Choose a Managed Website If…

…you want a professional site that stays current without your time.

What It Really Costs

Introductory prices are low, but renewals are often higher. Check the full renewal price before committing. With any do-it-yourself builder, the biggest cost is usually time. A few hours a month on updates, fixes and design tweaks adds up to several full days a year.

If You Want to Switch Later

Sites made with Hostinger's builder stay on Hostinger. Your domain can be moved, but the site would need rebuilding. Whatever you choose, make sure your domain name is registered in your own name. That's what lets you move without losing your web address.

Questions to Ask Yourself

  • Do I enjoy working on my website, or do I put it off?
  • When did I last update my hours, prices or photos?
  • Do I need to sell online, or do I mainly need customers to call, book or visit?
  • How much is an hour of my time worth to my business?

Going with Hostinger Website Builder? My Tips

  1. Check the renewal price, not just the first-year price
  2. Edit AI text so it sounds like your business
  3. Add real photos and reviews
  4. Set up your Google Business Profile alongside the site
  5. Test your contact form after publishing
